Why this offer exists

A rejection is not just 48 lost hours. It is a three-line boilerplate reason, a blind fix, a new submission, another wait. Some developers spin like that for a month.

Yet the vast majority of rejections come from a short list of known causes: a crash on first launch on a device you never tested, a missing or broken demo account, an inconsistent privacy questionnaire, an in-app purchase that does not restore, screenshots that do not match the app. All of it can be checked beforehand. That is exactly what we do.

It is the same review our students get. Students on the Capmedia Academy path get it before their first submission. This page is the version open to everyone.

The checklist

We check what the reviewers check.

On the app side

  • Stability: first launch, backgrounding, resume, rotation, on several real devices. The crash on launch is the dumbest and most common rejection reason.
  • The demo account: present, working, with real content in it. A broken demo login is an automatic rejection.
  • In-app purchases: a compliant paywall, purchase restoration, the mandatory disclosures, the subscription management button.
  • Permissions: every request justified by a clear message, no unnecessary permissions, account deletion within reach.

On the listing and paperwork side

  • Privacy: the App Store Connect questionnaire and the Play listing consistent with what the app actually collects, the policy online and up to date.
  • Screenshots and the listing: the right formats, screenshots that show the real app, copy without forbidden promises.
  • The age questionnaire and the metadata that triggers requests for justification.
  • The Google Play traps: the mandatory testers, the data safety declarations, the signing key.

What you get

Written feedback, point by point, within 7 working days.

For every issue found: the rejection reason it maps to, the exact Apple or Google rule involved, and the fix to make before you submit. For ambiguous points, the wording to have ready if the reviewer asks the question.

  • The list of blockers: what will get you rejected, to fix first
  • The list of risks: what often passes but can snag, with the counter
  • A written green light when it is ready: you submit with confidence

What it is not

  • A guarantee of acceptance: nobody can give one, Apple alone decides. Anyone who promises it is lying to you.
  • A full UX or security audit: for that, there is the mobile app audit.
  • A submission done for you: we can do that too, but it is a separate service, mention it in your message.

We would rather tell you what the offer does not cover before you pay, not after.

Questions

Before you decide.

›My app has already been rejected: is it too late?

Quite the opposite, it is the best time. Send the rejection message with your request: we decode the reason, check that your planned fix is enough, and go through the rest to avoid a second rejection on a different point.

›Apple only, Google only, or both?

Your call. The two stores have different rules and different traps: the quote clearly separates the two, and you choose.

›7 working days is too long for me.

Say so in your message: if the schedule allows it, an accelerated review is possible and the quote will say so. We never promise a deadline we cannot keep.

›Do you submit on my behalf?

We can, as a separate service: preparing the paperwork, the screenshots, the submission and the review follow-up all the way to "Ready for Sale". Mention it in your request and the quote will cover both.
